# Suggested Default Dimension Pairs

Reference document. The Phase 3 protocol offers these as starter scaffolding; sources decide what gets committed. The default is offered to accelerate Phase 3; it does not pre-empt source-driven derivation.

---

## How to use this file

In Phase 3 Step 1, identify which (if any) of the four pairs below best fits the dimension candidates that surfaced in Phase 2 Pass 2. Present both the suggested default and the source-driven proposal to the user at the Phase 3 STOP.

If the default and the source-driven proposal converge — name the convergence (strong signal).
If they diverge — present both and recommend the source-driven option.
If no default fits — derive dimensions directly from sources and document why the defaults were rejected.

---

## Pair 1: Engagement stage × Relationship type

### Engagement stage

How far along in the relationship with the organization the audience is.

- **Discovery** — Has not yet engaged. Recently encountered, learning what the organization is.
- **Consideration** — Engaging but not yet acting. Reading, attending, following.
- **Active** — Currently acting on the relationship. Donating, attending events, partnering, advocating.
- **Sustaining** — Long-term, recurring action. Multi-year donors, repeat partners, season-long fans.
- **Lapsed** — Previously active, currently not.

### Relationship type

What kind of relationship the audience has to the organization.

- **Individual** — Personal relationship; the person represents themselves.
- **Institutional** — Represents an institution (foundation, corporation, government agency).
- **Partner organization** — Represents a peer organization with overlapping mission.
- **Press** — Represents media; relationship mediated by audience reach.

### When this pair fits

Most general — useful for organizations whose primary audience question is about lifecycle stage and the kind of entity they're engaging.

### When this pair doesn't fit

When the organization's audience question turns on something other than relationship-stage progression (e.g., a sports league launch where every audience is at "discovery" stage; a research institute whose audience is uniformly institutional).

## Pair 2: Decision authority × Motivation orientation

### Decision authority

How the audience makes the decisions that matter to the organization.

- **Self** — The audience member decides alone.
- **Influenced** — The audience member decides but is meaningfully shaped by others (spouse, advisor, peer network).
- **Institutional approval** — The audience member proposes; an institution decides.
- **Committee** — A group decides collectively; the audience contact is one voice among several.

### Motivation orientation

What kind of motive drives the audience toward action.

- **Outcome-oriented** — Drives toward measurable change in the world.
- **Belief-oriented** — Drives toward aligning with values, identity, or worldview.
- **Belonging-oriented** — Drives toward community, affiliation, recognition.
- **Fiduciary** — Drives toward stewardship duty — managing on behalf of others.

### When this pair fits

Organizations whose primary audience question is about how decisions get made and what motives drive them. Strong for fundraising-heavy organizations, advocacy organizations, and B2B-style organizational engagement.

### When this pair doesn't fit

When motivation is uniform across the audience set and decision authority isn't a meaningful distinction (e.g., a consumer-facing entertainment property where every audience member self-decides on outcome-oriented basis).

## Pair 3: Channel × Information appetite

### Channel

Where the audience primarily encounters the organization's content.

- **Direct** — Email, mail, in-person conversation, phone.
- **Social** — Social media platforms.
- **Earned media** — News, podcasts, third-party publications.
- **Events** — Live events, conferences, gatherings.

### Information appetite

How much information the audience wants on a given topic.

- **Headline** — One line. Maximum signal-to-effort.
- **Explanatory** — A paragraph or short article. Enough to understand the why.
- **Deep-dive** — Long-form. Wants the full picture.
- **Data-and-citations** — Wants underlying data, methodology, primary sources.

### When this pair fits

Organizations whose primary audience question is about content strategy and channel mix — what kind of content to produce and where to deploy it.

### When this pair doesn't fit

When the organization's audience question turns on relationship dynamics or decision motivations rather than content consumption. (Channel/appetite is often better as a secondary dimension layered onto a primary relational dimension.)

## Pair 4: Proximity × Stake

### Proximity

How close the audience is to the organization's work.

- **Beneficiary** — The work directly affects them.
- **Beneficiary-proximate** — Close to those the work affects (family, community, neighboring populations).
- **Observer** — Aware of the work, not directly connected.
- **Beneficiary-distant** — Far from the work geographically, culturally, or experientially.

### Stake

What kind of stake the audience has in the organization's success.

- **Financial** — Money is at risk or in motion (donors, investors, customers).
- **Reputational** — Their professional or public reputation is associated with the organization (board, prominent advocates).
- **Mission-alignment** — Their cause-portfolio includes this work (peer organizations, advocacy partners).
- **Professional** — They work in or adjacent to the field (researchers, policy professionals, sector journalists).

### When this pair fits

Organizations whose work has geographic or experiential distance between the work and the audiences funding/supporting it (international development, conservation, advocacy for distant populations). Also useful when stake variation drives different content needs.

### When this pair doesn't fit

When proximity is uniform (e.g., a community organization whose audiences are all locally proximate) or when stake variation isn't meaningfully diagnostic.

---

## When no default pair fits

Derive dimensions directly from the sources. The Phase 2 dimension candidates document is the input. Phase 3's documentation of "why defaults were rejected" matters — the process log should make clear that defaults were considered, not bypassed.

Common reasons no default fits:
- The organization's audience question is unusual (a sports league launch, a closing-and-handoff engagement, an internal cultural change)
- The audience set is uniform along the default axes but varies meaningfully along a non-default axis (e.g., motivation type is uniform but trust state varies sharply between newcomers and established relationships)
- The sources reveal an organizational vocabulary for audience cuts that maps cleanly to a custom dimension pair the defaults don't capture

When deriving, the source-driven dimensions should still pass the same fitness tests:
- Does this dimension answer some part of the audience question?
- Do multiple sources support this cut, or is it derived from one source's framing?
- Would the cell content meaningfully differ across values of this dimension?
